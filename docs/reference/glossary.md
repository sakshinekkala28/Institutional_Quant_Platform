# Glossary

This glossary defines common terms, abbreviations, and concepts used throughout the **Institutional Quant Platform**. It serves as a reference for developers, quantitative researchers, portfolio managers, and infrastructure engineers.

---

# A

## Alpha

The excess return generated by an investment strategy relative to its benchmark after adjusting for market risk.

---

## Alpha Model

A quantitative model that generates investment signals based on market, fundamental, technical, or alternative data.

---

## Asset Universe

The complete collection of securities eligible for analysis and portfolio construction.

---

## ATR (Average True Range)

A volatility indicator measuring the average trading range of an asset over a specified period.

---

# B

## Backtest

Simulation of an investment strategy using historical market data to evaluate performance before live deployment.

---

## Benchmark

A reference index or portfolio used to evaluate investment performance.

Examples include:

- NIFTY 50
- NIFTY 100
- NIFTY 500
- S&P 500

---

## Beta

A measure of a portfolio's sensitivity to movements in the broader market.

---

## Black-Litterman Model

A portfolio optimization framework that combines market equilibrium returns with investor views.

---

# C

## Capacity

The maximum capital that a strategy can deploy without materially impacting market prices or execution quality.

---

## Covariance Matrix

A matrix representing the covariance between asset returns, used in portfolio optimization and risk estimation.

---

## CAGR

Compound Annual Growth Rate, representing the annualized growth rate of an investment over a specified period.

---

# D

## Drawdown

The percentage decline from a portfolio's peak value to its subsequent lowest value.

---

## Diversification

The practice of allocating investments across multiple assets or sectors to reduce portfolio risk.

---

# E

## Expected Shortfall (ES)

Also known as Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR), representing the average loss beyond the Value at Risk threshold.

---

## Execution Engine

The platform component responsible for converting portfolio decisions into executable trades.

---

# F

## Factor

A measurable characteristic used to explain differences in asset returns.

Common factors include:

- Value
- Momentum
- Quality
- Size
- Low Volatility

---

## Factor Exposure

The sensitivity of a portfolio to a particular investment factor.

---

## Factor Model

A model that estimates portfolio returns based on exposure to systematic factors.

---

# H

## HRP

Hierarchical Risk Parity, a portfolio optimization method using hierarchical clustering to improve diversification.

---

# I

## Information Ratio

A measure of active return relative to tracking error.

Formula:

```
Information Ratio = Active Return / Tracking Error
```

---

# M

## Market Regime

The prevailing market environment characterized by volatility, trend, and liquidity conditions.

Examples:

- Bull Market
- Bear Market
- Sideways Market
- High Volatility
- Low Volatility

---

## Maximum Drawdown

The largest peak-to-trough decline experienced by a portfolio during a specified period.

---

# P

## Portfolio Optimization

The process of determining asset weights that maximize expected return for a given level of risk or satisfy specified investment constraints.

---

## Position Size

The allocation percentage or capital assigned to an individual security.

---

# Q

## Quantitative Investing

An investment approach based on mathematical models, statistical techniques, and algorithmic decision-making rather than discretionary judgment.

---

# R

## Rebalancing

The process of adjusting portfolio holdings to restore target allocations or implement updated investment signals.

---

## Risk Parity

A portfolio construction methodology that allocates risk equally across portfolio components rather than allocating capital equally.

---

## Risk-Adjusted Return

Investment return measured relative to the level of risk undertaken.

Common metrics include:

- Sharpe Ratio
- Sortino Ratio
- Calmar Ratio
- Information Ratio

---

# S

## Sharpe Ratio

Measures excess return generated per unit of total portfolio risk.

Formula:

```
Sharpe Ratio =
(Expected Portfolio Return − Risk-Free Rate)
/ Portfolio Volatility
```

---

## Signal

A quantitative indicator suggesting whether an asset should be bought, held, or sold.

---

## Slippage

The difference between the expected execution price and the actual execution price.

---

## Sortino Ratio

A risk-adjusted performance metric that penalizes only downside volatility.

---

## Stress Testing

Evaluation of portfolio performance under hypothetical adverse market scenarios.

---

# T

## Tracking Error

The standard deviation of active returns relative to a benchmark.

---

## Transaction Cost Analysis (TCA)

Analysis of explicit and implicit trading costs incurred during execution.

---

# U

## Universe Builder

A platform component responsible for selecting securities eligible for investment based on configurable rules and filters.

---

# V

## Value at Risk (VaR)

An estimate of the maximum expected portfolio loss over a specified time horizon at a given confidence level.

---

## Volatility

A statistical measure of the dispersion of asset returns, commonly represented by standard deviation.

---

# W

## Weight

The percentage allocation of total portfolio capital assigned to an individual asset.
